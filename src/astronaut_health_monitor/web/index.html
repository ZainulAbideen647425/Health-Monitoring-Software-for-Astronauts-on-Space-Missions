    The five spaceflight hazards

    NASA's Human Research Program groups the risks of human spaceflight into five hazards. This page explains each one in plain words and says what this app does about it. It gives general information and does not diagnose anything.

    01

    Space radiation

    Radiation beyond Earth's protective environment can affect long-term health.

    What to watch
    Exposure records, new symptoms, mission environment.
    What to do
    Follow shielding and exposure-monitoring protocols. Report new symptoms to the flight surgeon.
    02

    Isolation and confinement

    A small crew in a small habitat can affect mood, sleep and relationships.

    What to watch
    Mood, sleep hours, fatigue, crew check-ins.
    What to do
    Keep a regular check-in rhythm and use the mission's behavioral-health support.
    03

    Distance from Earth

    Communication delay and limited access to care change how health concerns are escalated.

    What to watch
    Gaps in readings, worsening trends, communication windows.
    What to do
    Record changes early and use the mission escalation protocol. This app works offline for that reason.
    04

    Altered gravity

    Low gravity can affect the heart and circulation, bones, muscles and balance.

    What to watch
    Heart rate, blood pressure, activity, bone or muscle symptoms.
    What to do
    Complete the planned exercise sessions and report new bone, joint or back pain.
    05

    Closed environment

    A sealed habitat makes air, water, food and sleep conditions important for health.

    What to watch
    Sleep, nutrition, blood sugar, symptoms that follow habitat changes.
    What to do
    Follow environmental monitoring guidance and report notable changes.
